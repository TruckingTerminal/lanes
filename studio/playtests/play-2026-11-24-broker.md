# Play test 2026-11-24: the broker

Persona: a freight broker from the office side. I don't want to drive and I don't want to own trucks. I want to sit in
the middle, keep the spread and squeeze both ends. Laptop size, server lanes-player-3 (port 4183), build 2ce9b53.
Started Thu Jan 11, Year 1 in Chicago, owner-operator start. Reached Mon Mar 11, Year 2: about 425 game days.
About 130 tool calls, 20 screenshots.

## 1. The verdict

- I would not come back tomorrow as a broker. The brokerage itself, once I got it, was the most real thing in the
  game, but it cost me 14 game months of driving a leased truck on autopilot to reach it, and three weeks after I
  opened it my bond was full and I was boxed in.
- I'd come back next week if the door to brokering were a few months of hard work instead of a year of waiting.
- What the game is about, as I understood it: one shared freight market where you start behind the wheel of a leased
  truck and climb, and brokering is a second company you fund from your carrier once you're rich enough.

## 3. The diary

**Thu Jan 11 Y1, 06:00, first screen.** Title screen, one button: New test world. Good. Julia's opening line about
birthdays is fun. Then: "You start the way most do: one leased truck, a little money, and you driving." Fine, I'll
put up with it. Picked Chicago: rails and interstates, freight to anywhere, and a broker likes a hub. The city card
says "Shippers hold 56% of van loads for locals first". That's a nice touch.

**Company name.** The name field was prefilled ("Lakefront Hauling"). I typed "Middle Lane Freight", it seemed not to
take, I selected all and typed it again, and the field showed "Middle Lane Freight". The game saved it as
"Middle Lane FreightMiddle Lane F": both typings run together and cut at 32 characters. I only found that out later
on the Intel page. It's my name everywhere now.

**Jan 11, the board.** First look at the load board. A broker reads this screen like a load board should be read:
lane, all-in, rate per mile, empty miles, pickup and delivery windows, profit at book-now. Very good. Iowa City to
Millington at 7.96/mi against a market that "clears near 4.75/mi" was free money, so I booked it. The card tells me
the no-show claim (1,571 FD) and the late claim before I book. That is better than most real rate confirmations.

**Jan 11, first load booked: the menus open.** Staff, Dealer, Rates, Finance, Economy, Business, Back Lot,
Brokerage. I went straight to Brokerage: "Opens on Apr 2, Year 1. You'll need an active carrier and 75,000 FD for
the bond." So, 81 days and a lot of money. "See what brokers earned last month" led to the Intel page, where every
broker was ranked "#1" because no month had closed yet. Nothing to see.

**Jan 11, dispatcher.** Set the dispatcher to bid for me: market plus 5%, up to 300 empty miles. This was fiddly.
Julia's pop-up came in and moved the panel while I clicked, and I hit the wrong checkbox. Then I learned that the
on/off switch saves at once and throws away what you have typed in the other fields. I had to type 5 and 300
twice. In the end it stuck.

**Jan 11, Help: "Brokers and contracts".** Fair and clear: half the NPC freight goes to AI brokers at contract
rates that trail spot by about 30 days, and brokers take 6 to 12%. That's real. In a rising market contract brokers
get squeezed, which is exactly 2021.

**Jan 31 Y1.** January kept 6,772 FD. Cash 6,867. Thirty percent of every load is "held toward your truck". The
Finance page says the only loans are truck loans. No line of credit, nothing for a bond. At this rate 75,000 is ten
months off. Not good news for a broker.

**Late Mar Y1.** Cash 11,103, lease deposit full at 15,925 (so nothing more is held back). Julia now says buy a
truck. No thanks.

**Apr 2 Y1, brokerage opens.** Opened it at once. Step 1: "Opening a brokerage takes about 93,000 FD: the 75,000
bond, the licence, 15,000 to start trading and the 3% fee. Your carrier has 15,002 FD." And at the top: "Brokers'
market: freight per broker 1.0× the opening's, margins −9%: no room for a new brokerage." Step 2 lists the bond
(held), a surety premium of about 2,250 FD a year, and the 3% fee for moving my own money between my own companies.
Both "cash after" lines name my carrier; the second one should be the brokerage. "Not enough for the bond yet."

**Apr 2 Y1, what brokers earned.** The Intel page now shows the AI brokers' March: the best kept 2,266 FD, most
lost 1,000 to 4,600. My one leased truck kept 9,404. So the game's own numbers tell a broker: don't. I'm doing it
anyway.

**May Y1.** Cash 28,003. Found the Reserve: a holding cost of 2% a week on cash over 25,000 of working capital.
That tax lands on someone saving for a bond. Put 4,707 in the Reserve.

**Summer Y1.** "Preferred with Chicago shippers, tier 5": a lovely card with a lit-up truck stop. It says my Chicago
invoices now pay in 15 days instead of 30, but on a lease the lessor pays me on delivery, so I don't know what that
changes for me. The card stayed up through two of my waits; the clock ran behind it.

**Oct Y1.** October kept only 4,577 FD; taxes 2,765. Cash 61,296. The holding cost would now take 726 FD a week.
I moved 31,325 into the Reserve.

**Early Y2.** Cash 35,580 and Reserve 34,752, but the shelter cap fell to 14,736, so 20,015 of my "sheltered" money
was being charged anyway. My first 4,707 had become 4,613 at some point, and nothing said why. Saving here feels like
running up a down escalator. The Brokerage page now reads "freight per broker 4.0×, margins −3%".

**Thu ~Mar 7 Y2.** Cash 62,433 plus 33,944 in the Reserve: over 93,000 at last. Withdrew the Reserve; the first try
did nothing (I probably clicked outside the dialog), the second worked. Cash 97,538 the next day.

**Sat ~Mar 9 Y2, the brokerage.** Typed "Spread Desk Logistics" as the brokerage's name. Step 3, terms: I set 15%
margin ("Brokers here take 7%–12%"), Open vetting, up to 20 open loads. The confirm screen said "Start Middle Lane
FreightMiddle Lane F": my brokerage name never took, on two tries. I went back once to fix it, the clicks stopped
taking (background pane), and I gave in and started it under the carrier's name. Carrier left with 4,529 FD.

**The desk (about 20 days).** New tabs: BOOK, CARRIERS, MONEY, MORE. Within days: "Your brokerage has outgrown the
kitchen table. It has an office now." Loads were taken right away at my 15%: Houston to Broomfield, shipper 4,600,
carrier 3,910, fee 138, mine 552. Houston to Montgomery, mine 614. A small one, Jacksonville to Pooler, mine 77.
"5,152 FD margin this month" after about two weeks, about what my truck kept in a month.

**Mon Mar 11 Y2, the money screen.** This is a real broker's desk: 27 open invoices from shippers, net 30, each
with "Cash it now" at 8% plus 0.1% a day. And on the other side, "Owed by you", a list of named carriers with due
dates. Then the line that stopped me: "74,954 of your 75,000 FD bond in use." Brokerage cash 5,024. In under three
weeks my open payables filled the bond and new loads stop. The bond is not a safety deposit here; it's the
size of my desk. Nobody told me that in setup. I stopped there, at about day 425.

## 5. The three best moments, and the five things that most got in the way

Best:
1. The brokerage Money screen: receivables with a "Cash it now" fee on each line, payables to named carriers, due
   dates. For a minute I was back at my desk.
2. The first load card: the no-show and late claims spelled out before I book. Clearer than most real rate cons.
3. Reading the AI brokers' month on Intel: almost all of them losing money in a rising market on lagging contracts.
   That's how it really goes.

Got in the way:
1. A year of driving before I could broker, and the holding cost working against my savings the whole time.
2. My names: the doubled carrier name, and a brokerage name that never took.
3. The bond as a hidden cap on how much freight I can run, found only when it was full.
4. No lever for the broker to be a broker: I set one margin for everything. I don't pick loads, quote shippers,
   pick or rate carriers, or set a per-load rate.
5. The dispatcher form: the switch wipes your numbers, Julia's tips move the panel under your mouse.

## 6. What I never found or never understood

- Where a brokerage's freight comes from, and why my Chicago desk ran Houston and Jacksonville loads.
- The CARRIERS tab of the brokerage: I had no calls left to open it. I don't know if I can pick, block or rate
  carriers.
- What happens when a carrier is late on my load, when I pay a carrier late, or when a load falls off ("each costs
  you its fall-off fee"). I didn't see any of it.
- Whether my margin can be changed after opening, and whether "fee 138" on a load is the 3% settlement fee.
- The real-world gaps: here you post 75,000 cash and also pay a surety premium. In the US a broker posts either a
  75k trust (BMC-85) or buys a surety bond (BMC-84) for a few percent a year, not both. Real brokers set a rate per
  load and negotiate with carriers. A real bond is not a cap on open payables; your credit line and cash flow are.
- Why AI brokers lost money while my 15% loads were taken at once.
- What "freight per broker 4.0× the opening's" means for me, rather than for new AI brokers.
- Whether the Reserve's lost 94 FD was a fee or something else.

## 7. Then and now (against play-2026-11-10-middleman)

- Better: step 3 now gives context for the margin ("Brokers here take 7%–12%", and the fall-off fee range). The
  middleman's 90% went in with no word; my 15% at least came with a yardstick.
- Better: the brokerage can't go far into minus any more ("You can never owe carriers more than it"). The middleman
  ended at −17,358 cash; I ended at +5,024. The cost is that the bond now caps the desk, and it filled in three weeks.
- Better: the Intel page now shows what brokers kept each month, once a month has closed.
- Still there: before the first month, "See what brokers earned last month" shows every broker as "#1".
- Still there: name trouble. Then the brokerage prefill was cut mid-word; now my carrier name is doubled and cut at
  32 characters, and the brokerage name I typed never replaced it.
- Still there: CASH IT NOW at 8% plus aging. The page now says factoring is 5% flat, so at least the cheaper way is named.
- Not tested this time: the jammed transfer field, the carrier's dispatcher stalling while in the broker view, the
  load that paid its carrier the full shipper rate.
- New and worse, for this persona: the middleman started as a small fleet and had 99k by Apr 1. From the live
  world's owner-operator start, 93k took until Mar Y2, and the 2% weekly holding cost fights the saving all the way.
