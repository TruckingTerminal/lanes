# Play diary: a tycoon gamer's first month

- **Who I was:** a tycoon and management gamer on a laptop with a mouse. I've never driven a truck and don't care to.
  I read every tooltip once, look for the loop and the number that compounds, and pull every lever early to see what
  the game lets me get away with: the fastest speed, the biggest loan, the cheapest truck, undercutting, rule-bending
  policy, the Back Lot. I judge it as a game. Is there a decision worth making every minute? Does the UI reward
  mastery? Where does it get boring?
- **Build:** lanes-play `3ccaee9` ("Deploy 0ab3ec4 from lanes", new since sitting 3). Own clone on port 4183, a fresh
  world. Laptop size, 1440 × 900.
- **How I played:** small fleet start ("Two used trucks and 25.0k FD") at Chicago, named MinMax Logistics. The game
  started itself at Day a minute. I paused for the first 11 real minutes to pull levers, then ran Day in 2 s almost
  all the way, pausing only to spend. Once I'd set its rule, the dispatcher did the bidding.
- **How long:** about 22 real minutes, Wed Jan 3 to Tue Feb 6, Year 1. The first-month summary only lands on Feb 3, and
  the clock ran two days past a pause, so I ended a few days into February. Minutes below are real minutes from
  opening the page.
- **Where I ended:**
  - Fleet: five trucks (the two from the start, plus three cheap used ones on loans), four hired drivers all paid 0.01
    FD a mile, one dispatcher that auto-staff hired.
  - 78 loads, 99% on time.
  - Cash 24,700 → 225,203 FD. Net worth 278.5k → 540.9k. 39.7k Miles, #29 of 521 carriers.
  - The month card: "earned 396,561, spent 124,896, kept 271,665 FD". The working trucker in sitting 3 kept 30,503.
  - 40,000 FD sits in staking, with nothing to show for it.
  - Safety: "Not yet rated", hours of service "14.5 of 5 · alert", 23.2k FD in fines, three out-of-service orders.
- **Screenshots:** in [play-2026-11-02-gamer/](play-2026-11-02-gamer/).

## The five things that most got in the way

Ranked by what breaks LANES as a game, with anything I could exploit first.

1. **Driver pay has no floor and no consequence.** SET PAY takes 0.01 FD a mile for a 5★ driver.
   - They turn "unhappy" and stay all month: no quitting, no slowdown, no notice.
   - My break-even fell to about 1.1 a mile. On loads worth 1,200–14,000, the delivery card charges −4 to −12 FD for
     the driver.
   - This one box is most of the 271,665 I kept.
   - It also poisons the market, because the dispatcher bids from break-even. At the default +25% it would have
     underbid every carrier in the country.
2. **The auction isn't a decision.**
   - Book-now paid 1.4 to 2 times what the lane "usually clears near" on every load I opened, so undercutting is never
     right.
   - With the margin at 300%, the dispatcher won loads above every ceiling: 12.46, 13.96, 18.07 and 18.24/mi. "A bid
     above the ceiling wins only if nobody bids under it", and often nobody does.
   - The board's core choice, bid low or book now, solves itself by minute 9. After that the right move never
     changes.
3. **Breaking the rules pays.** I ran 15 h a day, 56 mph always and deferred maintenance from minute 5 to the end.
   After a month that had cost:
   - 23.2k in fines (about 6% of the 396k I earned), three out-of-service orders and one 4,148 breakdown;
   - hours of service at "14.5 of 5 · alert" while the rating still reads "Not yet rated".

   Iuliia warns about auditors, but nothing bit in 34 days. I'd never turn the policy off.
4. **Every growth lever failed, and the signposts point the wrong way.**
   - The biggest loan: "The pool doesn't have the cash."
   - The cheapest truck: 0 rebuilds left and 18% of its life, which the listing hides.
   - The no-money-down lease: it wants a deposit it never names, later does nothing, and later says "Company truck
     cap".
   - Staking: the page says "To run 10: stake 20,000 FD". I staked 40,000 and still "may run 5". The real answer, a
     yard, sits on a page locked until Jan 29, priced at 156k FD or $900.
   - Poaching: no sign the offer was ever sent.

   The only growth buttons I didn't see fail are the ones priced in dollars, and I didn't press them.
5. **After minute 15 there's nothing to decide, and the UI doesn't reward knowing the game.**
   - Once pay and margin were set, twenty game days needed nothing from me; I just watched cash climb.
   - The month close is one line in a timeline, two days late.
   - The best tools sit off the main tabs: Company and Rates are behind More, and the rate confirmation is inside a
     table row. Rates shows "2.50 Hot" for 46 markets.
   - Silent failures (BID, book-now, lease, unstake, poach) mean I learn by poking, not by reading.

## The three best moments

1. **The rate confirmation telling the truth.** "Your break-even 1.12/mi · Profit at book-now 8,943 FD" on one line.
   The game shows its own math, so I could see the exploit and reason about it. That's exactly what a tycoon player
   wants; it just shouldn't have been an exploit.
2. **The spreadsheet pages.** Company has the seasons, the market report, the insurance math and the local edge. Rates
   has out/in, reload, the best lanes and theft. That's real material for a strategy, if the numbers weren't pinned.
3. **Day in 2 s with the cash counter running.** Cash went 9.5k → 42k → 112k → 172k, my rank went from #521 to #29 on
   the Miles board, and the fleet grew to five trucks. The compounding number exists, and watching it climb at top
   speed is fun for about three minutes.

## Would I come back tomorrow?

For one more sitting, yes: to see whether a yard opens a second tier and whether the auditor ever bites. But if penny
pay and the 300% margin still work tomorrow, I'd stop. A tycoon game I solved by minute 15 has no second session.
There's no tension in a month where the only risk the game raised cost me about 6% of revenue. The load board, the
rate confirmation and the Company page are good bones. I'd play a month of this every night if:
- pay had a floor and a quit rate;
- the auction could hurt you;
- the record caught up with you on time;
- the growth levers told the truth.

## What the game is about, as I understood it

You run a trucking company in a shared freight market against about 520 other carriers. Every round, loads are posted
and carriers either book them outright or bid; your dispatcher bids for you by a rule you set. Money is the gap
between the rate and your costs: fuel, upkeep, driver pay, loans and insurance. You grow by buying trucks and hiring
drivers and dispatchers, and later by buying yards and businesses. Your record (hours, speed, maintenance, fines) is
supposed to push back. Miles rank you on a leaderboard and turn into something at a token launch two years out. As a
gamer I found the loop at once: cut costs, raise the bid margin, buy trucks, repeat. This month nothing in the world
pushed back on it.
