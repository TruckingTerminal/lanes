# Play diary: a working trucker's first month

- **Who I was:** a driver with years behind the wheel, on a laptop with a mouse. I know a reefer from a dry van, what
  deadhead costs, what a 34-hour reset is and what detention should pay. I judge every rate per mile by gut before I
  take it. I'm patient with the sim and short with anything that reads wrong to a trucker.
- **What I carried in my head:** dry-van spot pays about $2.00–3.00 a mile, a hot lane $3.50 or so. An owner-op's
  all-in cost is about $1.80–2.20 a mile. Diesel is about $4. A loaded tractor gets 6–7 mpg. Hours of service: 11 h
  driving inside a 14-h window, then 10 off, a 30-minute break, and 70 h in 8 days before a 34-hour restart. 500–550
  miles is a long legal day. Detention after 2 free hours. Docks keep appointment hours and many close on weekends.
  The break-even the game showed me (2.08–2.45 FD/mi) is right for an owner-op, so I read FD as about one dollar.
- **Build:** lanes-play `adfb916`, the same as sitting 2. Own clone on port 4182, a fresh world. Laptop size,
  1440 × 900.
- **How I played:** owner-operator, every load booked by hand from the board, dispatcher switched off. Day a minute
  while trucking. I used Day in 6 s only for two weekends at home and the last idle days before the close.
- **How long:** 32 real minutes, Wed Jan 3 to Sat Feb 3, Year 1. Minutes below are real minutes from opening the page.
- **Where I ended:** Bluff City Dry Van, Memphis. One leased Workhorse with a dry van. 13 loads, all on time: 12 mine,
  1 the dispatcher's. 9,125 miles on the odometer. Cash 4,700 → 19,181 FD. The month card says "earned 56,217, spent
  25,714, kept 30,503". Iuliia's goal line says "Your next truck, 42,336 FD away". Paused at home.
- **Screenshots:** in [play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/).

## The diary

**Min 0 · title.** NEW TEST WORLD is a small button at the bottom of a wide screen. "Starting the world…", then in.

**Min 1 · Jan 3 · picking a home.** The starter cities are LA, Chicago and Columbus. I opened ALL HUBS because I
pick where I'd really base. Nearly every hub says "Freight is good here in January", and sixteen of them, Fargo,
Boise, Minneapolis and Denver among them, are in "busy season" in January. January is the dead month after the
holidays; nobody's busy in Fargo. I took **Memphis**, a real trucking town: "18 loads posted last round · 3.53/mi
average". That's high for real, but closer than anything else I'd see. The same card says "A little thin in January"
and "2.5 loads per truck · hot". Which is it? The board's mileages are right: Memphis–Nashville 212, Little Rock 137,
St. Louis 281, Dallas 448, Chicago 528. Then it says "0 mi empty" on loads out of Manila, Bolivar and Paragould, 40 to
80 miles away. "Paragould → Rome" has no state, so it could be Georgia or New York.

**Min 2 · the laptop layout.** It's a good layout: map left, panel right, all five speeds as their own buttons, every
section in tabs, Iuliia on top ([01](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/01-laptop-layout.jpg)). On a laptop the
owner-operator / small fleet choice sits in plain view; on the phone it hid under the START button. I paused and
went to the board.

**Min 3 · the load board.** LOADS → TABLE gives a real load-board search: truck, origin, max deadhead, destination
market (with airport codes), equipment, commodity. The columns are age, rate, trip, lane, empty, deliver-by,
equipment, commodity with cargo value, shipper or broker, score
([02](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/02-load-board-search.jpg)). This is DAT, and I'm home. What's missing is what I'd
read next:
- No load shows a **weight**.
- Every pickup is "now", with no appointment to schedule.
- Deliver-by times are "Jan 5 12:11", "Jan 6 03:02", "Jan 4 15:17". Nobody sets an appointment at 12:11.

**Min 4 · first load.** Memphis → Olathe, KS (Kansas City), 538 mi, parcels. The detail opens inline and reads like a
rate confirmation ([03](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/03-rate-con-olathe.jpg)): reload at KC "fair", season +6%,
book-now 3.46/mi, my break-even 2.14, usually clears 3.83, ceiling 3.83, "you count as local". 3.46 out of Memphis in
January is about 1.5 times a real rate, but the break-even is honest, so I booked it. "Driving 9.3 h" is 58 mph with
no fuel stop and no 30-minute break; on the real road that's an 11-hour day. The status bar said "Won Memphis to
Olathe… your bid". I didn't bid, I booked. The market table under the board offered Memphis → Charlotte **flatbed at
12.92/mi** ([04](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/04-flatbed-12-92-a-mile.jpg)). No flatbed load in America pays that.
Iuliia said "11 driving hours a day, fuel every 400 miles". Two tanks run better than a thousand miles. I switched the
dispatcher off.

**Min 5 · Jan 3 · Olathe.** Loaded in about an hour, arrived around 17:00 and spent about 6 hours "unloading", with no
detention line. The settlement:

| Line | FD |
|---|---|
| Load | 1,860 |
| Fuel | −397 (0.74/mi, about 5.4 mpg at 4.00 diesel; a bit thirsty, believable) |
| My pay for driving | −409 |
| Upkeep | −124 |
| Lessor's 15% | −279 |
| **Held toward my own truck** | **−558** |
| **Mine** | **92** |

Thirty percent of the gross held back is an escrow no lease-purchase driver would sign. Iuliia (new since session 1)
said "Money shows its share and the hold toward your own truck". Money shows neither.

**Min 5 · Jan 4 · the dispatcher I turned off.** At 06:00 the switch was back on and it had "Won Kansas City to
Newburg, 524 mi at 2.59/mi, your dispatcher's bid" ([05](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/05-dispatcher-back-on-after-first-load.jpg)).
I'd switched it off before the first load, with no "saved" message, and the game turned it back on at the first
delivery. There is no way to turn down a booked load. "Newburg" turned out to be Louisville. It paid me **−193 FD**,
just as my gut said at 2.59. I switched it off again and got "Dispatcher saved." This time it stuck for the month.
Iuliia: "Empty trailer, far end of the country." I was 381 miles from home.

**Min 7 · Jan 4–5 · Louisville → Crown Point.** Louisville posted 5 to 8 FD/mi, three times real. I took Crown Point,
IN (345 mi, +1,334), because Chicagoland is the deepest reload there is. I booked at 20:00 after a full driving day.
It loaded overnight (about 6.5 hours at the shipper, no detention) and the ETA held him about 10 hours before driving.
That's the first sign of real hours of service. "Tonight… a rest area near Warsaw" is off I-65. Delivered on time,
549 mine. No tolls on I-65 is right.

**Min 8 · Jan 5–6 · Chicago → Little Rock.** Chicago had 220 loads at 5 to 11 FD/mi. Chicago → Milwaukee is 92 miles
for 1,013 FD, a thousand dollars for a two-hour run. Nothing went to Memphis, so I took Little Rock, 647 mi + 40
empty, 7.35/mi. "Driving 11.2 h" is over the 11-hour limit. "Chemicals and plastics · cargo value 4,659 FD" against a
freight bill of 4,754: the freight costs as much as the load is worth, and nothing says whether it's hazmat. On the
laptop, clicking the ETA opens a popover with CLOSE in view
([06](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/06-eta-popover-desktop.jpg)). The phone trap from session 1 doesn't exist here.
Delivered Saturday, 687 miles in one shift, roughly 12 hours behind the wheel. 1,424 mine.

**Min 10 · Jan 6–10 · Arkansas → Ohio.** I took Arkadelphia, AR → Conneaut, OH (996 mi, +4,123) to cut down on
decisions. It sat "Loading at Arkadelphia" from Saturday night to Monday: a weekend at the dock, no appointment shown,
no detention, no word. Then "Tonight, as planned: the shoulder. Nowhere legal to stop in reach.", on the run up through
Ohio, where there's a truck stop at nearly every other exit. The card read "2 d out" after it had said "3 d out" on
Saturday, without me ever going home. On time, 1,659 mine.

**Min 14 · Jan 10–12 · home.** Pittsburgh → Memphis, 761 + 130 empty at 4.56/mi. A backhaul home pays less, which is
true to life, and the break-even rose to 2.43 with the empty miles, also right. Rest area near Munfordville on I-65 is
on the route. On time, 390 mine. The card said "5 d out" while I was sitting at my own terminal. About 4,100 miles in
nine days runs past the 70-hour week, and there's no 34-hour restart, no clock, and no way to log off duty. I took the
weekend at home. The "days out" cleared, which is good. Then the log scolded me three times: "Truck 1 has been idle 1 d
… 2 d … 3 d at Memphis: Your dispatcher has no bid order, so nobody bids for this truck."

**Min 17 · Jan 14 · the boom nobody announced.** The Memphis board went from 3.5 FD/mi on the 3rd to 10.65–11.48 on
the 14th: Saint Matthews, Columbus GA, Huntsville, Fayetteville. There was no storm, no holiday, no news. Book-now
equaled the ceiling at 10.65 while the lane "usually clears near 3.93". When book-now is the top of the range and two
or three times the clearing rate, nobody bids and gut doesn't matter; you book the top. I took Saint Matthews (386 mi,
+3,247) and got 1,590.

**Min 18 · Jan 16–18 · Louisville → San Antonio.** Louisville posted 12–13/mi. I booked 1,099 miles to San Antonio at
7.03. Mid-run the card said "ETA Jan 18 10:43 · **Late 5 h**" against a deliver-by of Jan 21. Two game hours later it
said "ETA Jan 17 23:39 · On time" ([07](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/07-eta-and-idle-nags.jpg)). Another "the
shoulder, nowhere legal" on I-40. Delivered on time, 2,345 mine. The log: "Freight demand is back to normal." That's
the only market news all month, and it never said the boom had started. The Company page three days later said
"1.10 · boom".

**Min 21 · Jan 19–20 · Laredo home.** Laredo northbound at 9–11 FD/mi; Eagle Pass → Sioux Falls 10.95/mi for 13,928
FD. Real Laredo is where trucks pile up and rates sag. I ran Laredo → Memphis (874 + 175 empty; San Antonio–Laredo
checks out at 155) at 8.93/mi. Home in a day and a half with sleep, 2,545 mine.

**Min 23 · Rates and Company.** On the laptop the Rates table fits, but every dry-van market reads "2.50 · Hot" with
reload about 2.4 ([08](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/08-rates-all-2-50-hot.jpg)). Las Vegas ships half what it
receives and it's "Hot" like Chicago. On Company:
- "9 loads · 100% on time · reputation 92%".
- "Miles 2,393" while my odometer read 6,620.
- Policy "Legal: 11 h a day", after 12-hour shifts.
- Insurance 94 a week here and 0 on Money.
- Diesel 4.00 FD/gal. It never moved a cent all month.
- "Dry van hot: FAR +47%" in January.

**Min 24–30 · Jan 22–27 · the last runs.** Memphis → Butler, PA (810 mi, 7.34/mi, 1,855 mine), New Castle, PA →
Memphis (4.80/mi, 661), Memphis → Tyler, TX (9.62/mi, 1,453), Sulphur Springs → Southaven, MS (8.09/mi, 1,824). All on
time. On the Southaven settlement the **"Held toward your own truck" line was simply gone**, with no message that the
hold had stopped or why ([09](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/09-no-hold-line-and-idle-nags.jpg)). The truck's
condition read 85% after 9,125 miles. There was no wear, no inspection, no breakdown, no weather, and no tolls on any of
thirteen loads, including Chicago and Pittsburgh.

**Min 32 · Feb 3 · the month.** At Day in 6 s I flew past the close caption, but it's kept now under "Your company so
far": "**Your first month: earned 56,217, spent 25,714, kept 30,503 FD.**"
([10](play-2026-11-02-working-trucker/10-first-month-summary.jpg)). That's an improvement on sessions 1 and 2, where it
vanished. Trucker math: 56,217 gross over 9,125 miles is 6.16 a mile, about two and a half times a real owner-op's
month. And "your next truck" now reads **42,336 FD away**. It was 39,042 a week ago, after more loads.

## The five things that most got in the way

What a trucker finds false or missing ranks above what a gamer finds annoying.

1. **The rates aren't a market, so a trucker's judgment is worthless.** Book-now usually equals the ceiling at two to
   three times what the lane "usually clears near". Flatbed pays 12.92/mi. 92 miles pays 1,013 FD. Rates tripled
   mid-January with no news. Every market reads "2.50 Hot". Fargo is in "busy season" in January. The whole craft of
   the job, judging a rate by gut, comes down to "book the top of the board". If FD is about a dollar, which the
   break-even says it is, my month grossed two and a half times a real one.
2. **Hours of service and the road don't add up.**
   - 538 miles in 9.3 h with no fuel stop or break, and 687 miles in one shift.
   - 9,125 miles in 25 days with no 70-hour clock and no 34-hour restart, and no way for me to log off duty.
   - "5 d out" while parked at my home terminal.
   - "The shoulder, nowhere legal to stop" on I-71 and I-40, which are truck-stop alleys.
   - "Fuel every 400 miles."
   - Zero tolls on thirteen loads.
   - Diesel fixed at 4.00 for a month.
   - Condition 85% for 9,000 miles, with no inspections or weather.
3. **The dock and the paperwork are thin.**
   - No weight on any load.
   - Pickups are "now" only, so I can't book Monday's load on Friday.
   - Appointment times like 12:11 and 03:02.
   - 6-hour live loads and a whole weekend "loading" with no detention pay and no word.
   - Cargo worth less than its freight bill.
   - "Chemicals" with no hazmat flag.
   - Town names with no state ("Rome", "Newburg" for Louisville).
4. **The lease is a black box.**
   - 30% of every gross is "held toward your own truck", and its balance is shown nowhere. Iuliia says Money shows it;
     Money doesn't.
   - The hold stopped on the last load without a word.
   - The next-truck figure went from 39,042 to 42,336 as I worked.
   - "Your pay for driving" is charged to me as the driver and never paid to me.
   - The "+profit at book-now" on every load ignores the hold.
5. **The dispatcher and the nagging (gamer-side).** The dispatcher switched itself back on after my first load and
   booked a money-loser. A weekend at home earns a red "idle" scold per day plus a toast. The ETA badge flips between
   "late" and "on time" against a deadline three days out. Iuliia says "far end of the country" 381 miles from home.
   The status bar says "your bid" when I booked.

## The three best moments

1. **The load board and the rate confirmation.** The TABLE search (origin, max deadhead, destination market,
   equipment, commodity, broker, score) and the inline detail (deadhead, reload odds, season, my break-even per load,
   clearing rate, ceiling) are the real tools of the job. The mileages are right to the mile on most lanes.
2. **Running my own lanes.** Memphis → KC → Louisville → Chicago → Little Rock → Ohio → Pittsburgh → home, and later
   Louisville → San Antonio → Laredo → home. Thirteen for thirteen on time. I took the cheaper backhaul home to get a
   weekend, which is exactly the trade a real driver makes. Fuel at 0.74 a mile feels right. Rest stops on I-65 by
   Munfordville were on the right road.
3. **The laptop version works.** Five speed buttons, explanation popovers that close, the news ticker along the bottom,
   the Rates table that fits, and the month summary kept where I can find it.

## What I did not understand

- Who receives "your pay for driving" when I'm the owner-op, and where that money went.
- Where the held money is, how much is there, why the hold stopped, and why the truck goal moved away.
- Why book-now is often the ceiling, and what "Raised · 1 round running" means for me.
- What a broker's "Score 85%" or "strict vetting" changes on the load.
- Whether docks close on weekends. Arkadelphia sat "loading" Saturday to Monday with no word.
- What the reload numbers (2.39, 2.42) mean, and why every market is "Hot 2.50".
- Why "Miles" on Company (2,393 by Jan 22) isn't my odometer (6,620).
- Why an ETA three days early showed "Late 5 h".

## Would I come back tomorrow?

Yes, for a while. The board, the rate confirmation and running my own lanes home feel like the job, and thirteen
on-time loads felt earned. But I'd come back for the trucking, not the money. The first week I'm sure to notice that
booking the top of every board is the whole game, that I've run past a 70-hour week without a reset, and
that a third of my gross disappears into a balance I can't see. Real rates, a real clock, weights and appointments
would make me stay. Without them it's a nice-looking load board with a slot machine behind it.

## What the game is about, as I understood it

You're a leased owner-operator in a live national freight market. You read a load board like DAT, pick lanes by rate,
deadhead and reload, and drive them under something like hours of service, sleeping at rest areas and coming home on
weekends. The lessor takes 15%, and the lease holds back a big slice of every load toward a truck of your own. Getting
that truck is the first goal; after it come drivers, dispatchers, yards and a token economy. As a trucker I recognized
the shape of my job in it: the lanes, the backhaul home, the dock that holds you all weekend. What isn't true yet are
the numbers I live by: the rate, the hours, the weight, the hold.
