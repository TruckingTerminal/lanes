## Verdict

Batch 7 passes the gate on all ten seeds, the first time windows have, and it closes the brokers' leak: the carriers
receive 94% of the cap's freight pay (93% off), the brokers' fall-off is 1.5% of it (1.9% off), and their share of the
pay falls from 73% to 15%. But windows still cost the carriers a fifth of their pay: the cap pays 18% less for freight
than with windows off (1,144M against 1,395M over four years), the mean rate is 7% lower, cleared loads 6% fewer, and
the companies spend less, so the budget refills less. Owner-operators' liquidations rise from 0.19 to 0.32 of the free
path, companies bankrupt from 179 to 244, loan defaults double, and FD in circulation ends at 72M against 118M. The bots
also buy 385 facilities a run and close all but three: a churn the facility test's one-week memory makes. The newcomer's
month at Dallas is level with today's. My recommendation: windows stay out of a ruleset until one more batch lifts the
carriers' pay back to the off arm's range, with two changes sized in §4; the gate alone would let them in, and I would
not.

## 3. The four lines, read

1. **The carriers' share: met (94%).** Pricing the brokers' contracts on the memory-free index ended the brokers'
   leak: the memory now lifts what the board pays carriers and not what the shipper pays the broker, so the brokers
   take what they can cover.
2. **The brokered share: 15%, not near half.** The brokers' contracts sit under the board's lifted rates, so carriers
   leave the brokered loads for the direct ones, the brokers' bonds fill with posted loads nobody hauls, and from there
   half of all broker-only tenders go to the board as spot freight (tenders to the board 348,000 against 234,000 off).
   Not a leak (the fall-off is small, the freight moves as spot), but the brokers become a small trade under windows,
   and 31 of 60 still go under.
3. **The fall-off: met (1.5% of the cap's pay).**
4. **Facilities: 385 bought a run, 2.6 standing.** The fallback now reads the hub's NPC lost share; a bot buys where it
   is low, the facility's own loads are lost at their close, the bot closes it at eight weeks, and once its loads stop
   the hub's facility tally has nothing to read within the week, so the fallback says "buy" again. 20M of purchases a run
   go to the facility dealer (a sink that refills the budget) for nothing built. **The cover rule never fires (0 passed
   up)**: with the brokers' share at 15% no hub's AI brokers reach four takes and a quarter lost in a week; it is a
   backstop that the pricing made unnecessary.

## 4. What is left, and the next sizes

The carriers' pay is the miss: the cap pays 18% less for freight, the rate is 7% lower, the budget is underspent by 156M
over four years (paid 1,144 against a budget of 1,300) and the volume lever sits at the floor only 42 days. The cap is not
what holds the pay down; the price is. Without windows a load that does not clear walks 10% a round for up to ten rounds
and clears at the walked price, and the cap pays that; with windows the memory lifts the next posting at the hub (at most
×1.87) and the load itself walks four steps in its last two days. What clears under windows clears near its opening
price, and the opening is still below where the off world's loads end. Two changes, behind the same switch, for batch 8:

1. **The memory's lift steeper, not higher.** The lift is the hub's ratio to the power `rates.ratioExponent` (0.35);
   under windows the ratio sits at 1.5–3 at most hubs and the lift is ×1.15–1.47. A windows exponent of 0.5
   (`windows.ratioExponent`) puts it at ×1.22–1.73 for the same ratios, about the 7% the rate is short across the
   board, with the clamp's top unchanged. A two-seed check first: the rate and the cap's pay against the off arm.
2. **A facility closed at a loss blocks the hub for the bots for 26 weeks** (`windows.facilityBlockWeeks`), so the next
   bot does not rebuild where the last one failed; the tally's week is too short to remember.
