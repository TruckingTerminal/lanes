# Early delivery: what the wait costs (director, 2026-11-10)

**Ruling: C, with guards.** The default follows it.

"Never invented lost earnings" meant a forecast the game makes up: an assumed rate, a guessed market. A trailing
average from the company's own books is a fact about its past, computed and checkable. Said as the past, with the
condition in plain words, it doesn't cross my line. And it's true out there: a fleet pays to expedite because a
truck that isn't there earns nothing.

## The guards
- **Only for an order that adds a truck to a fleet.** Never on a lease replacement (the leased truck still earns;
  the lessor's share is the whole cost), never on a trailer alone.
- **Only when the truck would have a driver.** The company must have a driver without a truck, or an owner who
  drives it. A truck with nobody to drive it earns nothing, so it counts nothing. Then Iuliia says "You'll need a
  driver for it first" instead of pricing earnings.
- **Kept per truck, from the books:** after the driver's pay and the truck's costs, trailing over the last eight
  weeks (the economy may set the span), floored at zero. Nothing while the company has under four weeks of history.
- **Said as the past, never added into a promise.** The payments and the earnings are two figures, each named
  for what it is.
- **The same rule for the bots,** with the cushion still holding after the fee.

## Iuliia's sentence shape
> Waiting four weeks costs about 4,400 FD in payments. Your trucks have each kept about 2,200 a week over the last
> eight weeks; if this one does the same, that's about 8,800. The fee is 6,900.

Then one of:
- "It pays if the freight holds."
- "It doesn't pay at your recent weeks; I'd wait."

**The fixed parts:** the payments first, as the certain cost; the earnings as "have kept … if this one does the
same"; the fee last; the verdict conditional on the freight. The writer may tune the words, not that order or the
"if".
