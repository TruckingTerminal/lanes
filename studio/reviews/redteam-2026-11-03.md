# Red team pass, 2026-11-03

The studio's red team (studio/roles/redteam.md). An adversary with a login, run as the last step before a
republish and once a day while rules change. Run against the checkout at HEAD d879cab (the brief named b5b48e6;
another session had advanced it, nothing of mine on main); ruleset 0.19.2-phase0.31, config version
0.19.3-phase0.32, the live levers of `packages/config/src/live.ts`. The target is beta one: the token is in
beta (it launches on in-game day 730, `token.betaYears` 2 × 365), the crews are off (`robbery.enabled` false),
carrier theft is off (`fraud.theft.byCarrier` false), and the pools stand down under the factoring contract.
This pass covers factoring (`token.payment.factoring.contract`), the population dial (`population.dial`) and the
lock discounts (`token.staking.lockDiscounts`) on in turn and together, plus the rules already live.

Nothing on main changed. The red team's strategies live under `apps/sim/src/redteam/` (new, mine) and its runs
under `runs/redteam-*` (gitignored). No commit. Typecheck clean; the probes drive companies only through the
public `applyAction` and read the ledger — they change no engine code.

**Headline.** No mechanical exploit takes FD out of the beta economy: all six economy probes come back at
0 FD/day, because the sinks are sinks (they refill the reserve, never mint) and every escrow — the bond, the
contract stake, the lease deposit, the loan stake — is matched by a payment or a return. The holes that remain
are the ones the design already names as waiting for the server: **multi-account (sybil) farming of the airdrop
and the logbook**, which no client-side rule can close. The fuzzer found two genuine engine breaks — one live in
the beta today (an escrow goes a few cents negative), one latent at launch (the spend-only sub-ledger does not
round-trip through the stake/bond escrow).

## 1. Game the economy

`apps/sim/src/redteam/exploits.ts` builds a live-profile world, turns each switch on, and either extracts FD or
names the guard. `apps/sim/src/redteam/harness.ts` is the shared builder and the invariant reader. Seed 1 shown;
seeds 4 and 8 reproduce it.

| Probe | Switch | Result | What stopped it (FD/day) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E-LOCKS** lock-discount farming | `lockDiscounts` | **closed** | A small lock on a big purchase: discount capped at the lock's budget (50 FD on a 182k rig from a 5k 1-month lock, not 1,820). Break-early: discounts kept 24,000 − slash 58,000 = **−34,000 FD**, always a loss. 0 FD/day. |
| **E-FACTOR** factoring / quick-pay loop | `factoring.contract` | **closed** | 200 game days: the NPC factor advanced 86.8M FD, discounts to `sink:factoring_fee` 5.0M, **NPC paper unpaid 0, from overdraft 0**. The advance is always matched by a payment; the discount is a sink that refills the reserve. No faucet. |
| **E-BONDWASH** broker owes past its bond | `factoring.contract` | **closed** | 250 days, 30 brokers: 12,562 AP-cap refusals, the bond paid the NPC factor/lessor 313k, **NPC paper unpaid 0**. The cap holds a broker's payables ≤ its posted bond and the bond pays NPC paper first, so a wound-up broker never stiffs the factor (no reserve drain). |
| **E-DEPOSIT** lease-deposit credit | live rules | **closed** | 160 days of free starters buying their first truck: deposits held 671,937, returned 671,937; cap identity 0.00 throughout. The credit is savings released into the purchase, in/out net to the reserve, never minted twice. |
| **E-USEDLOT** buy used cheap, flip | live rules | **closed** | A lot rig costs 46,949 and is priced at its own used value and worn; listed back, the market paid nothing over 30 days → −46,949. No cheap-buy-and-flip. |
| **E-DIAL** dial as a faucet / bailout | `population.dial` | **closed** | 180 days with a join wave so the dial acts: cap identity held with the dial on and off; the write-off faucet is **no larger** with it on (2,443 → 2,443). The reserve differs between on and off only because the carrier count differs (entrants trade) — the dial moves carriers, not money, and bails nobody out. |

**The attacks the brief named, and where each landed:**

- **Wash loads between an own broker and an own carrier against the bond cap** — E-BONDWASH. The AP cap
  (`receivables.ts apCapBlock`) refuses any tender or booking that would push a broker's payables over its posted
  bond, and the bond pays the NPC factor/lessor before any carrier and before an overdraft, so washing freight to
  your own carrier cannot pull FD the factor won't get back. No leak.
- **Factoring and quick-pay loops** — E-FACTOR. Factoring advances are NPC-to-NPC on shipper paper and
  bond-backed on broker paper; the discount is a sink. There is no loop that mints.
- **Locking, buying at the discount, breaking early** — E-LOCKS. The per-lock budget caps the discount, and the
  slash (2× discounts used + 10% of the lock, burned) always exceeds what the lock gave.
- **Splitting a fleet across companies for the airdrop or the discounts** — **this is the real hole, see §1a.**
  For the *discounts* it does nothing (the lock budget is per lock, not per company, and with `lockDiscounts` on
  there is no size tier to dodge). For the *airdrop* it is the whole game.
- **The deposit credit and the used lot** — E-DEPOSIT, E-USEDLOT. Both closed.
- **The warm start's first rounds / the dispatcher's idle window** — not an FD exploit. The two-day warm start and
  the first-bid-by-hand rule (`freeEntry.firstBidByHand`) give a newcomer a richer or a one-day-later board; a
  one-truck company's truck waiting for the owner's first bid costs it idle fuel, not the world FD. No extraction.
- **Staged insurance claims and self-robbery** — **not reachable in the beta.** `fraud.theft.byCarrier` is false
  (a carrier cannot steal the load it hauls) and `robbery.enabled` is false (no hired crews, no self-robbery,
  no fence). The fuzzer still exercised `rebroker_load` thousands of times with no leak. When the Back Lot comes
  to a later beta these two vectors must be re-run against `robbery.lot`.
- **The refusal reasons the config hints at** — the fuzzer (§2) checks every refusal against the state; no action
  was accepted that the state forbade, and no refusal was issued that the state allowed to pass as `ok`.

### 1a. The holes that remain: multi-account farming (the server's job)

The engine has no same-owner, device or verified-login check — the design names this as not built
(TOKENOMICS.md "Anti-sybil"; `token.ts` counts one company per account). So one person running N claude.ai
accounts is N companies, and every per-company allowance multiplies:

1. **Sybil airdrop farming — the top hole.** The airdrop pot is split pro-rata by Miles and beta profit, capped
   at 1% per company (`token.airdrop.capShare`). N wallets each earn an allocation and each hit the 1% cap, so a
   cluster collects N% of the pot instead of one company's share, and each allocation's spend-only FD becomes
   withdrawable through its own logbook. The design's own bad-actor batches put numbers on it: in v51 (the full
   launch run, ten sybil clusters of eight) **sybils took $3–12k and airdrop farmers $11–18k of real dollars a
   run**, against operators who came out even. As FD it is the share of the pot a cluster carries off; a 10M pot
   at the 1% cap is 100,000 FD a wallet.
   *Smallest fix:* the spec's identity layer — verified login, device and same-owner detection at the bridge, so
   a withdrawal is gated per verified person, not per account. Nothing in the lockstep client can do it.

2. **Logbook volume-milestone wash-farming.** The logbook unlocks the spend-only airdrop through milestones;
   the volume ones ("25 / 100 / 250 / 1,000 loads handled — delivered, brokered or shipped") count any load.
   `robbery.logbookMinVictimTrucks` (3) gates only the *criminal* hit-milestone, not the volume ones. Two wallets
   you own (a broker and a carrier, or two carriers) can wash loads between themselves — the broker posts, your
   carrier hauls, both score the load — to clear each other's volume milestones and free the spend-only airdrop
   for withdrawal without doing real freight. The take is the airdrop each wallet unlocks early.
   *Smallest fix:* count a load toward a logbook volume milestone only when the poster and the hauler are not the
   same owner (an arm's-length-counterparty rule), which again needs the server's owner link; until then, raise
   the volume-milestone weights' reliance on the *delivered-on-time* and *tenure* milestones, which a wash can't
   fake cheaply.

3. **Starting grants per account.** Post-launch a new company's 5,000 FD grant is locked (`token.lockGrants`), so
   it can't be cashed out directly — but N accounts is N free NPC leases and N grants of working capital. Small
   next to the airdrop; the same identity fix closes it.

These are launch-era and were measured by the design's v39/v46/v51 batches; this pass confirms the engine still
offers no client-side defence and re-states them as the ranking's top entries.

## 2. Break the engine

`apps/sim/src/redteam/fuzz.ts` feeds well-formed random actions (built from the world's real ids, so they pass
`badInput` and exercise state) from three attacker companies — a free owner-operator, an established fleet and a
broker — over a few hundred game days, and after every injected action and every stepped hour checks: the ledger
balances (`assertBalanced`), no escrow or allocation account is negative, locked FD never exceeds a solvent
account's balance, the cap identity `minted = supply + reserve + allocated + burned` holds, every refusal is
true to the state, and the whole run replays bit-for-bit from the same seed and step log.

**Clean where it counts.** Money is conserved (the double-entry ledger never threw; the cap identity held to the
cent), every run replayed identically, no action threw, and no refusal was false. On the pure beta live profile,
seeds 1–3 were clean over 400 days (≈19,200 steps each). Two breaks surfaced:

### HOLE E2-EXIT — the unstake-queue escrow goes a few cents negative (live beta, today)

- **Seeds:** live fuzz seeds 4 and 5 (`fuzz.ts --seeds 1,2,3,4,5 --days 400 --actions 50`). Seed 5 at day 76,
  seed 4 at day 310: `co:exit:<id> is negative (−0.03 FD)`.
- **Shortest path:** a company queues an unstake (`unstake_fd` with `instant:false`), then the daily exit queue
  (`staking.ts dailyStaking`) clears it in partial slices under the global exit limit. `const fd = Math.min(e.fd,
  room)` is posted out of `co:exit:<id>`, but `e.fd` and `room` are floats while the ledger holds cents; across
  repeated partial clears (and the 25%-haircut split on the instant path) the float bookkeeping drifts a cent or
  two past what the escrow actually holds, so the post overdraws `co:exit:<id>` to −0.03 FD.
- **Severity:** tiny in magnitude but a true invariant break (an escrow account must never be negative), and it
  is reachable in the live beta right now through ordinary stake/unstake — the only one of the two that bites
  before launch.
- **Smallest fix (`staking.ts dailyStaking`):** cap the payout at the escrow's real balance —
  `const fd = Math.min(e.fd, room, w.ledger.balance(exitAccount(c.id)))` — and settle the instant haircut as the
  remainder in cents so the two posts sum to the staked amount exactly.

### HOLE E3-LOCK — the spend-only sub-ledger does not round-trip through the stake/bond escrow (latent, launch)

- **Seeds:** launch fuzz (`fuzz.ts --seeds 1,2,3 --days 400 --actions 50 --launch`), reproduced with
  `--trace` on seed 2 at day 35: broker `co:84` reads `locked 4,674.32 > balance 4,376.90` (over-locked by
  297 FD); seed 1 ended at `co:88 locked 74,987 > balance 53,580`.
- **Mechanism:** post-launch, a broker's kept working capital is spend-only (locked). It re-posts its 75,000
  licence stake (locked FD moves into `co:stake:<id>`), the bond pays its carrier and factor paper out of the
  stake (`receivables.ts drawBond`), and the licence stake is later unstaked back to cash
  (`co:stake:84 → co:84 75,000`). The FD ledger stays perfectly conserved (the run replayed identically and the
  cap identity held), but the *locked* sub-balance that rides along on these escrow postings does not come back
  equal to what left: after the round-trip the company's `locked` sits a few hundred FD above its actual cash.
- **Severity:** what the fuzzer saw is an *over-lock* — harmless, because `free() = max(0, balance − locked)`
  clamps to 0, so the company can withdraw *less*, never more. The danger is that the same non-conservation can
  run the other way (an *under-lock*), which at launch would let spend-only airdrop/grant FD be withdrawn through
  the bridge as if it were free. The bridge is not live in the beta, so there is no exposure today; it must be
  closed before the token launches.
- **Smallest fix (`ledger.ts`):** make the locked sub-ledger conserve across every `post` the way the balance
  ledger does (carry `moved` symmetrically into escrow accounts and back), or add a `reconcileLocks()` that sets
  each `co:` account's `lockedCents = min(lockedCents, balanceCents)` after the daily escrow steps, and assert
  `lockedSupply ≤ supply` as an invariant.

Everything else held: no negative stake or company-escrow beyond the two above, replays matched on every seed,
and the identity never drifted.

## 3. Attack the live page

Read from `apps/web/src/live.ts` and `apps/web/src/game.ts` (and README §"The live world"). **There is no game
server.** The world is a seed in `world/meta` plus every player's action log in the artifact's shared store; the
deterministic engine (`dmath`, no wall clock, no `Math.random`) turns that log into the same world on every page.
The README says it plainly: *"pages trust each other's logs."* That trust is the whole attack surface.

**What a modified client can submit to the shared store:**

- **Backdated and rewritten actions — a time machine.** The honest client stamps each action with
  `at = max(prev.at, ceil(gameMinute) + LEAD_MIN)` and a rising sequence `s`. `parseActs` only checks that `s`
  and `at` are non-negative integers and the body is < 8 KB. A hacked client can write any `at` (including a past
  minute) or re-use a sequence number with a different body. An action dated before a minute already computed
  makes every other page set `needRebuild` and replay the log with the injected action folded into history
  (`ingest` / `rebuild`). So a player can **insert an order into the past** — bid on a load knowing how the round
  cleared, or rewrite a losing decision — and every page will adopt it, because a rewritten own-log entry is
  resolved by *taking the store's version* (`ingest`: same seq, different body → `needRebuild`, store wins).
  Nothing a permanent, no-undo world promises survives this.
- **Acting as, or flooding, its own log.** A client writes `players/<uid>` and holds the `players/<uid>/x/tab`
  lease. It can write arbitrary (well-typed) actions at any rate; `applyAction`/`badInput` validate the *fields*,
  but not the *timing* or the *right* to act then.
- **Acting as another company — only if the store's rules let it.** The code assumes each `players/<uid>` doc is
  writable only by that `uid` and `world/meta` only by the owner, but those are store access rules, not something
  the client enforces. If the store does not bind writes to the authenticated `uid`, a client can append actions
  to another player's log, or reseed `world/meta`. The client cannot defend this; the store rules must.
- **Sybil.** One company per `uid` is enforced on replay (`apply` ignores a second join for a uid), but one
  person with N claude.ai accounts is N companies with no owner linkage — this is the §1a hole, surfaced here as
  the client cannot see it either.

**What stays safe without a server:** the engine is deterministic and the ruleset is pinned (`RULESET`), so a
page on different rules refuses to replay rather than fork history, and a well-formed action can't crash a page
(the action boundary and the fuzzer both confirm it). `world/meta` is owner-only *if* the store enforces it.

**The rule list a server must enforce once it exists (the spec's Postgres + workers):**

1. **Stamp the effective minute server-side.** Reject the client's `at`; assign the minute on receipt. No action
   may be dated before the current clock — the log is append-only, so no backdating and no rewriting a committed
   entry (reject a re-used sequence with a changed body).
2. **Authenticate the writer.** A player may append only to its own log, as its own company; `world/meta` and
   another player's log are read-only to it.
3. **Re-validate every action authoritatively** with `badInput` + `applyAction`'s state checks on the server's
   copy of the world (the client's `ok` is advisory).
4. **One company per verified person**, with the spec's verified-login, device and same-owner/sybil detection —
   this is what closes §1a's airdrop and logbook farming, and it is the only place it can be closed.
5. **Gate withdrawals (the bridge) on the server**, applying the early-sale tax, the daily cap, the frequency and
   impact fees, the logbook locks and any case hold — and fix E3-LOCK first, so spend-only FD can never leave as
   free.

**The one fact Jeff must know:** with no server, the shared world is only as honest as the players' own logs, and
a modified client can rewrite its own past — a no-undo, permanent world is not actually permanent for a motivated
player until the authoritative server stamps minutes and owns the log. (This is already in the README's "Trust"
note; the red team confirms it is the single largest integrity gap, above every economy knob.)

## Ranking (by what it hands an attacker)

| # | Hole | Take | Smallest fix | Routes to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sybil airdrop farming (no identity link) | ~100k FD / 1% of the pot per extra account; v51 measured sybils $3–12k, farmers $11–18k a run | the server's verified-login + same-owner/device check, gating the bridge per person | the spec / engineering (the server) |
| 2 | Logbook volume-milestone wash-farming (own broker↔carrier or 2 sybils) | the spend-only airdrop each wallet unlocks early without real freight | arm's-length-counterparty rule on volume milestones (needs the owner link); lean the weights on delivered-on-time and tenure | economy designer + the server |
| 3 | Modified client backdates / rewrites its own action log | rewrites history in a no-undo world (bid on known outcomes, undo losses) | the authoritative server: server-stamped minutes, append-only, per-identity writes | engineering (the server) |
| 4 | E3-LOCK spend-only sub-ledger non-conservation through stake/bond escrow | latent: an under-lock would let spend-only FD leave via the bridge at launch | conserve `locked` across escrow posts, or `reconcileLocks` + assert `lockedSupply ≤ supply` | engineering (ledger) |
| 5 | E2-EXIT unstake-queue escrow goes −0.03 FD (live in the beta) | a few cents of escrow overdraft per clear; breaks the no-negative-escrow invariant now | cap the exit post at the escrow's balance; settle the haircut in cents | engineering (staking) |

Every economy switch of today's three — factoring, the dial, the lock discounts — holds: 0 FD/day extracted, the
cap identity intact, no faucet. The work for a republish is the server (holes 1–3) and the two ledger fixes
(4–5); none is a reason to hold the beta, since the bridge and the airdrop are post-launch and E2-EXIT is cosmetic.
