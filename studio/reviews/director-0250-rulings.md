# Three rulings from the 0.25.0 replays (director, 2026-11-13)

## 1. One deadline: on time means by "Deliver by" (rule; switch, economy pair, next ruleset, restart while empty)
The claim, the reputation mark, and the on-time share in the standing scorecard all read latestDeliveryT. The old due
time retires with the explanation the guide gives for it.

**Why:** a mark the player never saw on the card is a deadline they couldn't see coming, and that fails the test
we've used all along: cutthroat, but visible. A dispatcher who delivered inside the time printed on the load and
still got "late" would call the game false, and they'd be right. "Deliver by" already carries the commodity's own
slack, so it is the shipper's deadline, and there's no second, stricter one hidden behind it. With the on-time mark a
quarter of the bid score and the basis of standing, two meanings of "late" would rank carriers on a figure nobody
is shown. Do it now: the live world has no players, so the restart is cheap, and it only gets dearer.

## 2. The home is the home market, at the radius the engine already uses (rule as it stands; one fix)
Springfield, 45 miles from Columbus, is home. The home is the same 150-mile radius that counts a night at home.

**Why:** a driver whose truck stands an hour from his house goes home; nobody drives him to the hub first. One radius
for every home rule keeps "home" meaning one thing.

**The fix:** home time starts at once when a past-due driver stands idle inside the radius, not at the next round, and
the card says so: "Ana is home (Springfield, Columbus market): 5 days off from now." A Send home control is offered
only outside the radius, where it means something.

## 3. The home rule is the driver's; moving the truck is the dispatcher's (rule)
With the dispatcher off, the game never drives a truck home.
- A past-due hired driver refuses loads that lead away from home until sent home.
- An owner-operator's fatigue builds.
- Both are warned from five days ahead, as my ruling C has it.

**Why:** the dispatcher card is right, "every truck you don't handle yourself", and nothing happens to a company
that it didn't set up. Being due home is a fact about the driver. Running 1,167 empty miles to answer it is a
dispatch decision, and with the dispatcher off that decision is the player's. If C went live with 0.25.0, this
deadhead is a bug against it, not a new question.
