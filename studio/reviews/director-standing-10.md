# Standing with a shipper, ten tiers (director, 2026-11-13)

Your four points stand, with three additions that make it truer: tender acceptance, a volume floor per tier, and
standing earned only where the shipper actually sees the carrier.

## The score (rule)
A shipper's scorecard, as real shippers keep them, over a trailing window (the economy sets it; I'd start at 90 game
days):
- **On-time:** the share of pickups and deliveries on time, weighted by loads.
- **Volume:** loads hauled for the shipper. Contract loads count double; short hops count less, so nobody farms
  standing with cheap runs.
- **Tender acceptance, from tier 7:** a first call declined or left to lapse counts against the carrier, as a
  rejected tender does out there.
- **Against it:** claims, no-shows and cancels, each a fixed hit that stays in the window.

**Each tier also has a floor on volume and on on-time,** so one perfect load never reaches the top. For example:
- tier 5: 15 loads and 90% on time;
- tier 9: 60 loads and 95% on time.

The economy sizes the figures.

**Only on the shipper's own direct and contract loads.** A load through a broker builds no standing with the shipper,
because out there the broker holds that relationship. Standing with brokers is a later layer (backlog).

## Movement (rule)
- **Reviewed once a week,** at a fixed game hour. The tier moves at most one step, up or down. That's the answer to
  Jeff's variance point: one bad delivery or one lucky week never swings it.
- **A heavy hit is felt as a run of weeks,** not one cliff. A no-show keeps pulling the tier down a step a week
  until the score recovers.
- **Decay:** four weeks with no load for the shipper costs one tier, and so on down to tier 1. A month away costs a
  tier, not five.
- **Kept at the company:** never sold, transferred, or shared between one owner's companies. It goes with a
  liquidation. A new company starts at tier 1 with every shipper. Bots follow the same rule.

## The milestones (rule; the economy sizes their effect)
| Tier | Unlocks |
|---|---|
| 3 | Dock priority: a better appointment, less dwell |
| 5 | Net-15 instead of net-30, or quick pay without its fee |
| 7 | First call on the shipper's tenders, one round ahead of the board, limited to loads the truck can make |
| 9 | Contract offers and renewals |
| 10 | A dedicated-lane offer: a standing weekly volume on one lane |

**Between milestones,** the tier orders who gets first call and who is offered a contract first. A higher tier means
first in line, so every step counts for something.

## The names (words)
Numbers with three bands, so ten steps read at a glance:
- **Approved,** tiers 1–4
- **Preferred,** tiers 5–8
- **Core,** tiers 9–10

## The shipper's card (words)
> **Kessler Foods · Preferred, tier 6 of 10 · rising**
> Next: first call at tier 7, about 3 weeks at this pace.
> 94% on time over 90 days · 22 loads (4 on contract) · 1 claim

- **The trend:** rising, steady, or slipping, from the last four weekly reviews.
- **The detail figures** carry an explain key: how the score is built and what moves it.
- **The month report** lists every tier change, with its cause.

## Iuliia at each milestone (words; the writer polishes)
- **3:** "Kessler's docks will take you ahead of the queue now. Less waiting at their doors."
- **5:** "Kessler pays you in 15 days now, not 30. That's your cash two weeks sooner."
- **7:** "Kessler calls you first now: their loads reach you a round before the board. Turning one down counts
  against you."
- **9:** "Kessler wants you on contract. Their offer is in Money."
- **10:** "You're one of Kessler's core carriers. They've offered you a dedicated lane."
- **Falling through a milestone, with the cause:** "Kessler moved you to tier 6 after two late deliveries. First call
  is gone until you're back at 7."
- **Decay ahead:** "You haven't hauled for Kessler in three weeks. One more and you drop a tier."

None of this belongs in the first hour. The first line a player hears is the one I gave before, at their first
on-time delivery for a shipper with a scorecard.
