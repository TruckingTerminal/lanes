# Three economy ideas: the player-facing side (director, 2026-11-12)

The test for each is pillar 1: a dispatcher should recognise it as true. The mechanics are borrowed as ideas; the
names, the look and the words are LANES's own.

## 1. The transaction burn → "the settlement fee"
**The name (words):** "Settlement fee". Out there, every payment between companies clears through someone who takes
a cut: factoring, quick pay, payment platforms. A trucker reads "settlement fee" as a cost of being paid, not a tax.
Never "burn" on a player's screen; that word belongs to the token pages.

**Where it shows (words):**
- **The pay line,** as its own line: "rate × miles, less fuel, less the lessor's 15%, less the settlement fee 0.5%
  (32 FD), equals yours."
- **The receipt and the sale confirm,** the same line in FD.
- **The month report's costs,** as one row, "Settlement fees".
- **The public report (§2),** as the total gone from the economy.

**The explain key (words):** "Every payment between companies clears through the freight payment network, which keeps
0.5%. No company receives it: it leaves the economy, which keeps FD scarce. Everyone pays the same rate; it's
published in the monthly report."

**What keeps it from reading as a skim (rule):**
- **One fee, not a stack.** Where a sale already pays a market fee, the settlement fee folds into it as one named
  line. Never three deductions for one sale.
- **The same rate for everyone,** bots included.
- **A change is announced a game month ahead** in the report and on Intel, and takes effect only with a ruleset.
- The size and which flows it touches are the economy's and Jeff's.

## 2. The monthly economic report
**Where and whose voice (words):** on Intel, "The freight economy · March, Year 2". It's written in a plain
trade-paper voice, figures first, no jokes. It's a publication, not Iuliia. She adds one line above it on what it
means for this player's freight: "Dry-van rates in the Southeast are up 9%; that's your lanes." It replaces the
"Economy tab" the cut list held back for a published monthly report.

**The order (words):** freight people first, the token last.
1. **Freight:** loads posted, cleared and lost; the rate index by equipment and region; the share delivered on time.
2. **Trucks:** the new and used price indexes, trucks bought, scrapped (with the value scrapped), repossessed.
3. **Companies:** started, liquidated, the count in service. Aggregates only.
4. **Money:** FD in circulation, what came in by kind, what went out by kind (the settlement fee among the sinks),
   the net.
5. Next month's announced changes, if any.

**What keeps it true and private (rule, privacy):**
- **Aggregates only.** No company named, no ranking by cash or by anything that reveals cash.
- **A minimum group size.** Any figure drawn from fewer than five companies (a thin hub, a rare equipment type) is
  folded into the next larger group or left out.
- **Published at a fixed hour after the month closes,** and never revised silently. A correction is printed as a
  correction in the next report.
- **Bots count as companies,** since they are in the market.

## 3. Shipper loyalty → "standing with a shipper"
**My view: not a points store.** Out there, shippers keep carrier scorecards. A carrier earns its place in the
routing guide by delivering, and loses it by failing. Nobody buys a perk with points plus cash. A store bought with
FD is pay-to-win and false.

**Standing, earned and lost (rule):**
- Earned on that shipper's loads delivered on time, contract loads counting more.
- Lost on late deliveries, no-shows, cancels and claims.
- It decays when a carrier stops hauling for the shipper.
- **It's the company's own.** It can't be sold, transferred, or shared between one owner's companies.
- Tiers, not a balance: Approved → Preferred → Core.

**What a tier gets, all true to trucking (rule):**
- **First call on the shipper's tenders** for one round, like locals-first. Limited to loads the truck can make
  (canMake), so standing can't hoard a board.
- **Better pay terms:** net-15 instead of net-30, or quick pay without its fee.
- **Dock priority:** a better appointment, and less dwell.
- **Contract offers and renewals** at the core tier.

**What it never gets:** an insurance discount (that comes from the safety record, from insurers) or a rebuild voucher
(that comes from the dealer). Neither comes from a shipper out there, so neither does here.

**Guards against pay-to-win (rule):** no tier, perk or standing is bought with FD or with money. Bots earn standing on
the same rule. If Jeff wanted this as a sink, it isn't one, and I'd keep it that way. The sink is §1.

**How a player learns it (words):**
- Iuliia at the first on-time delivery for a shipper with a program: "Kessler Foods noticed. Keep delivering on time
  and they'll call you first."
- The shipper's card shows the tier and what the next one gets.
- The month report lists standing gained and lost.
- What now? may prefer a load that keeps or builds standing, in tier 5 (a load to book).
- Not in the first hour.
