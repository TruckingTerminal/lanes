# Director's rulings (2026-11-02, sprint 0)

Routed by the producer from the UX spec (draft 2, "Questions") and the writer's copy audit. Each ruling is
final for the studio unless Jeff overrules it through the producer.

## 1. The first board promises nothing the market doesn't (UX question 2)

Live, a bid clears at the next round, up to three real hours away, so a stranger can't be promised a win inside
five minutes without planting a load. We don't plant loads: pillar 1 says the market is real, and a board that
lies once to a newcomer is a board a dispatcher stops trusting. The target changes instead. The bible now reads:
a stranger has a bid in inside five minutes, knows when it clears, and understands what it paid. "I made a
call" happens at the bid; "it paid" is the next sitting, which is the core loop, not a failure of it.

What the first board must do is make a profitable choice *possible*: a new authority at its home hub should see,
most rounds, at least a few open-vetting loads out of that hub with a positive number on the card, and the
locked ones dimmed with the reason. Today Dallas shows none. That is a question of who may take what (the
90-day authority gate, local-first shares, how many direct shippers post at a starter hub), which the economy
designer sizes; it is not a planted load. If the sim shows the first board usually has no profitable open load
for a new authority, the gate moves, the market doesn't.

Book now stays on every card at its true profit, red when negative; Counter is the primary action, as UX
proposed. In the test world, where the clock can run, the win still comes in minutes.

## 2. Three starter hubs (UX question 4)

Chicago, Dallas, Phoenix: three characters a dispatcher reads in one line each (freight piles up and pays to
leave; the middle of everything; thin freight out, locals get first call). One condition: each must pass the
first-board test above in the sim. If Phoenix fails it, Memphis takes its place. "Change" still opens the full
hub list, so nothing is lost for the player who knows what they want.

## 3. Where Iuliia's Russian line is (writer's audit, "Davai")

Iuliia may name Russian things, people and places, and never say Russian words: if a reader needs Russian to
get it, cut it. So the samovar, the Moscow fleet, the nineties and the grandmother stay; "Davai" goes, and
"Go on" is right. That is what "by bearing, never a costume" means, and it leaves room for the allusions Jeff
asked for.

## 4. The starter hubs after the first-board test (economy review, 2026-11-02)

**Dallas, Los Angeles, Phoenix.** A starter hub is a promise to a stranger, and at the live clock Chicago's May
is a real week every quarter in which the hub we recommended offers nothing. That is the market, and the
market stays; it just doesn't get to be our default. Chicago stays one tap away under "Change", and every hub
card, in that list too, carries the season honestly ("freight is thin here this month"). One condition: the
economy designer runs Los Angeles through the same ages as the other three and checks a starter's first weeks
at its 3.0× theft risk. If Los Angeles fails either, the three are Chicago, Dallas, Phoenix with that seasonal
line on Chicago's card.

## 5. The economy's two proposals for sprint 1

1. **Two game days before the page opens: yes,** for the live restart and for every new test world, since the
   test page is where sprint 1 is judged. An older world is an honest world; a planted load is not.
2. **The profit chip on the dispatcher's break-even: yes,** on one condition: the chip, S3's "Your break-even"
   row and S7's pay line use the same arithmetic, and S3 names the expected empty run as an expectation. One
   number told three ways is the slop we are cutting.

The vetting gate stays where it is: agreed with the economy designer, not worth a newcomer's exposure.
