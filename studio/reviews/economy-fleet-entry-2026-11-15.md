# Economy: AI fleet growth and entry by conditions, the director's ruling 16 (2026-11-15)

For the director's §16 (director-sprint-2026-11-15.md), the second half of §3. It goes to Jeff with the brokers'
proposal (economy-broker-entry-2026-11-15.md).

**The study:**
- A scratch build in a scratch worktree at 4625eae (lanes-fleet), on 0.25.2's live profile.
- Two seeds, four years, and the four questions the director asked.
- **The tightness signal:** cleared loads per truck in service over 8 weeks, against the opening's weeks 5–12. Posted
  loads per truck swing with lost postings, so cleared loads read the market better.
- **The health signal:** the bot carriers' trailing profit per truck-week.

## The answer

**The carrier side doesn't need entry rules for its size. It sizes itself to the freight, and it recovers from a hype
bust by itself.**

**The fleets' fall is a share shift, not a hollowing out.**
- Fleets go from 54% of the trucks to 37% by year 4 because owner-operators take what they lose: 400 → 445 trucks,
  against fleets' 465 → 312.
- Total trucks hold at about 840–920 in every version.

**Growing the fleets by conditions holds their share, but at the owner-operators' and newcomers' expense, so it
crowds out the players.**
- Fleet growth is mechanical: fleets keep 45–48% of the trucks.
- Owner-operators run 17–24% fewer trucks, newcomers earn 6–10% less a truck-week, and the market is no bigger.
- Fleets' own profit per truck-week falls 18%, because the growth comes on leased trucks into thinner freight.

**My recommendation is principle yes, rule not yet.** The same principle (renewal by conditions) answers the brokers,
whose layer did empty. For the carriers, the market's own buying already renews the layer. A fleet rule would only
move share from owner-operators to fleets.

If the director wants the fleets to hold the top of the standing ladder and the contract board, that is a share goal.
A rule that reads the fleets' share of the carrier layer, not freight per truck, would do it. Its cost would come out
of the owner-operators' and newcomers' trucks, as here.

**What each version shows:**
- **1. Growth before entry.** Fleets lease a truck when the market is tight and their own trucks clear the next
  truck's overhead. That holds their share, and every truck they add is one an owner-operator doesn't run. With the
  tighter rule (1′: ×1.15, and the fleet itself earning 1,500 FD or more a truck-week), it grows less but the same way.
  The tightness signal sits at 1.13–1.21 throughout, because the market's total doesn't change, so it never turns
  growth off.
- **2. Entry, small.** New NPC carriers on the free path, no grant, with up to two lessor leases (one to three
  trucks). 24.5 open in four years and about half fail. They cost no grant: their trucks are the lessor's on its own
  terms, the deposit held back from settlements, so the faucet's cost is nothing, against the brokers' 3.6M. They add
  churn, not capacity.
- **3. Restarts.** Winding a failed fleet up instead of restarting it (on the first probe, with growth and entry)
  counted 171 bankruptcies against 160.5, with the fleets' trucks unchanged (403 against 390). Freight refills
  through the others either way, so a fleet counted once tells the truth in reports at no cost to capacity. It's the
  same choice as the brokers' wind-up.
- **4. A hype bust.** No newcomers for the year after launch (days 760–1125). The newcomer flow, the beta's model of
  people arriving, starts only at launch (about day 730).
  - Newcomers' trucks: 4.5 at year 3, against 60.
  - The incumbents grow into the freight: owner-operators 519 trucks at year 3 against 478, fleets 382 against 338.
  - All trucks at year 4: 902 against 843.

  **The carrier side recovers by itself:** the bots buy trucks when their trucks pay. The director's worry that a
  bust leaves no way back doesn't hold in the sim.

## Passes and fails, against the director's criteria

- **"The fleets' trucks follow the freight instead of only falling":** with growth, yes (390–420 against 312 at year
  4). But the freight per truck never falls, so they follow it up by taking owner-operators' trucks.
- **"The gate, bankruptcies and the newcomer's month inside the spread":**
  - Bankruptcies 141.5–153 against 139, and newcomers earning 6–10% less a truck-week, on two seeds: probably at the
    spread's edge.
  - The newcomer's month can't move: the rule waits for 12 weeks of the market before it reads anything.
- **"The faucet's cost beside the brokers' 3.6M":** nothing. Entrants and growth run on the lessor's leases.
