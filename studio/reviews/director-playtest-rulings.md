# The guide's path, bids, people, money: rulings on the 2026-11-10 playtest round (director)

The finding stands as stated: the guide is honest about each number and wrong about the path. The fix is one
instruction at a time, from a ranking, and never a step that can't be done.

## The guide's path

**A. The first-truck card: one verdict. Words, plus one figure from the economy.**
This amends my 2026-11-09 calls, which priced the cheapest truck whatever its wear and trailer and let Iuliia warn
against it. Three worlds show that reads as two instructions.
- **Which truck she prices:** the cheapest *sound* truck in the company's freight that the page shows.
  - "Sound" is a figure, not a taste: a condition at which expected repairs stay under a level the economy sets
    from its wear rules.
  - Worn trucks and trucks for other freight stay on the list for those who look, named as such.
  - The card's name says what it prices: "To a sound first truck: 519 FD".
  - If none is listed: "No sound dry-van truck on the lot today; I'll tell you when one lists."
- **The tests, in order:** the truck exists and is shown → the cash on the day → the cushion afterwards.
- **One verdict from them,** and only one:
  - "Buy it now."
  - "Not yet: buy at 9,400 FD in cash, about 3 loads from here."
  - For a queued truck: "Order it now · ready May 23".
- **The supporting figures sit under "Why", never beside the verdict.** That covers the price, the cushion, the
  lessor's share while waiting, and the worn alternative.
- **The Dealer opens on this same verdict.**

**B. The dispatcher in the walkthrough. Words. The first-bid-by-hand rule stands (Jeff's yes).**
- **The step right after the first hand-booked load is "Set your dispatcher before you go".** The bible's first ten
  minutes already ends there; the walkthrough lost it.
- **Iuliia's line:** "It bids for your truck while you're away. You can still book by hand any time."
- **What now? names it** whenever a truck idles with the dispatcher off and the player has been away.
- **For the economy (sprint 4's guide bot):** hand play that follows What now? must be able to match the
  dispatcher. If it can't, the guide's advice is the problem, not the player. Most of this diary's loss was the
  home rule's deadheads (C), not the bids.

**C. The 21-day home rule with the dispatcher off. Words now; my view on the rule for Jeff.**
- **Words now:**
  - What now? warns from 5 days ahead: "Due home Jan 31 (5 days). A load toward Los Angeles keeps the truck
    paid: [load]."
  - On the day: "Due home today: take a load toward home, or Send home."
  - It outranks walkthrough steps (F).
- **My view on the rule:** with the dispatcher off, the game tells and never drives. Nothing happens to a company
  that it didn't set up, and an owner-operator decides when they go home.
  - If the owner ignores it, fatigue keeps building (my 2026-11-10 fatigue ruling); a hired driver past due
    refuses loads away from home until sent home.
  - With the dispatcher on, it plans homeward loads before the due day, as now.

**D. Steps 7 to 13 for a one-truck company. Words.**
**The rule:** a step is shown only when it can be done. Its page must be open and its object must exist. A step
that can't be done yet waits, neither skipped nor shown, and What now? shows the next one that can.
**The order for a leased one-truck owner:**
1. The dispatcher (moved up, B)
2. Miles
3. The law
4. The first truck (A's verdict)
5. On owning: the condition bar and the rebuild
6. On owning: the yard
7. Hire a driver, only when a second truck is ordered or a truck has no driver

Never the condition bar on a lessor-maintained truck. Never "a second truck needs a second driver" with one truck.

**E. "While you were away". Words.**
- **Every return after a day or more away shows exactly the time away,** in this order:
  1. What went wrong: lost loads, claims, deadheads, breakdowns, repossessions, a missed payment.
  2. The money: delivered, paid, kept, cash then and now.
  3. On time, as a percentage.
  4. One next step.
- **If the absence crossed a month's close,** a link to that month's report.
- **Iuliia's line is drawn from the worst item, then the best, of that interval.** The same line never shows twice
  in a row.

**F. What now?'s order of attention. Words.** Across all trucks, the highest tier wins; within a tier, the nearest
deadline:
1. Losing an asset or going under: a repossession, liquidation, or cash below zero before the next pay.
2. Money bleeding now: an idle or stranded truck, or a driver paid with no truck, by cost a day.
3. A load at risk: pickup or delivery claims.
4. Due home, or condition near a breakdown.
5. A load to book.
6. A walkthrough step.
7. Follow-ups.

A lost-bid follow-up names its truck and expires at the next round's clear, or when the load is gone. The debt
line names the nearest clock: "1 payment missed: the lender takes Truck 2 on Jul 28 if the next one misses."

## Bids and prices

**G. The floor and the default. Words.**
- **The floor is break-even itself.** The player may type under it, with the loss said: "Under break-even: you'd
  lose 140 FD."
- **The default bid follows my 2026-11-09 rule:** the usual clear, never over the ceiling, never under break-even.
  It's shown only when the lane has enough clears; otherwise there's no default.
- **"Bid on…" is recommended only when a default exists.**
- **When loads like this clear under the player's break-even:** "Loads like this clear near 2.10 here, under your
  break-even of 2.21. This lane pays less than it costs you; book another or pass."

**K. A broker's margin. Words.** Before the margin is saved, the sheet shows it in figures and against the market:
"At 90%, carriers see 10% of the shipper's rate, about 300 FD a load. Brokers here take 12–20%. Loads no carrier
takes fall off, and each costs you its fall-off fee (184–688 FD on recent loads)." The figures come from the
world's own clears. Iuliia adds nothing beyond it.

## People and trucks

**H. Booking through home time. Rule; my view.** Out there, a driver on home time isn't called out.
- **A hired driver:** booking is refused unless the pickup is after the days off end: "Ana is home until Thu 06:00;
  this pickup is Wed 14:00." Booking ahead past that is fine.
- **An owner-operator may cut their own days off,** warned first: "You'll go back out with 2 days at home; your
  days-away count won't clear."
- Nothing silent, ever.

**I. The owner's seat. Rule; my view: now, not the backlog.**
- The owner chooses the truck they drive, or none ("owner off the road").
- **The day's staffing never leaves a paid driver without a truck while a truck stands empty,** the owner's
  included. If the owner's truck is gone, they're told and offered the seat choice.
- A paid driver sitting beside an idle truck is money bleeding (F, tier 2).

**M. The standing orders' defaults. Words: keep them off.** Turning the dispatcher on is the setup; each further
order is a choice the player makes when it matters. Iuliia offers each one at its moment, once:
- after a second stranding: "move idle trucks";
- at the first contract offer: "take contracts";
- after a load lost between rounds: "book between rounds".
