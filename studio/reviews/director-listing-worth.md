# A truck's worth, the sell sheet, and a certain exit (director, 2026-11-10)

On play-2026-11-10-exploit-hunter.md and studio/reviews/economy-listing-worth.md.

## 1. The card: both figures, and the second promises only what's true
"Worth" goes; it said a price nobody pays, which breaks pillar 1's test.
- **First figure: "Book value 171,526 FD".** Its explain key says what the lender and the lot price it at: its list
  price today less miles, age and wear. It rises with new-truck prices.
- **Second figure, in FD, never as a ratio,** worded by how sure it is:
  - **Inside the starters' band (under about 60k):** "Usually sells for about 40,500 within two weeks."
  - **Above it:** "Few buyers at this price. Fleets pay about 103–137k for trucks like this, when one is buying;
    it can take weeks, or not sell."
  - **Once a certain exit exists (§3):** "The dealer takes it today for 111,500", and the market line beside it.
- **"Would sell for" isn't used until a figure is near-certain.** A range with "when one is buying" is honest;
  one number above the band isn't. Once the world has its own used sales, the range comes from them, not the
  economy's estimate.

## 2. The sell sheet: that set, in this order
1. **The price path:** "The price drops 5% a week, to 60% of today's book value (95,700) in week eight."
2. **The cost of waiting:** "A listed truck pays the idle fee, 20 FD a day, while it waits."
3. **The end:** "At 95,700, if no one has bought, it comes off sale and is parked."
4. **Above the starters' band, the buyer line:** "Only carriers with the cash buy trucks this dear; there are few."

The listing card carries "Take it off sale" from day one. Iuliia's line on the sheet compares the paths in
figures when there is more than one (list, a dealership's 80%, the dealer's 65%).

## 3. My view for Jeff: yes, a certain exit at wholesale
**Out there, a truck nobody wants at retail always has a wholesale price:** a dealer's offer or an auction.
"You can always sell to the dealer, at a wholesale price" is true. A truck that literally can't be sold is the
false part. It also keeps the risk where pillar 3 wants it: the downside is the 35% off book, which is a real loss,
not a frozen asset paying idle fees for ever. And it gives the "would sell for" line one figure it can always
promise.

**The guards I want:**
- **The loan comes first.** The price repays the loan on the truck. If 65% doesn't clear it, the player may pay
  the difference from cash; otherwise it's refused.
- **Only an idle truck:** no load on it, none booked ahead. It's handed over where it stands.
- **Never inside liquidation.** Liquidation keeps its own rule.
- **Every way out is shown together.** A dealership's 80% stays above it and is shown first when one is open; the
  player chooses.
- **No round trip:** the economy checks that buying from the lot, from a floor listing or new, then trading in,
  always loses after fees and tax. If any path doesn't lose, it's closed before the switch goes on.
- **Bots:** the same rule. The ten-seed pair reads the lot's stock, the listings, the starters' first truck and the
  gate, as the economy proposes.

Iuliia never pushes the trade-in; she prices it against listing ("Listing may get you 120k in a few weeks, less about
600 in fees; the dealer pays 111,500 today").
