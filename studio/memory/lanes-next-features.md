Decided by Jeff on 2026-10-28, to build next (after a context clear):

1. **Hand-dispatching always beats the best hired dispatcher.** Plus a detailed tutorial and a docs/wiki section so players learn the market (seasons, reload odds, bidding) and can out-dispatch any AI.
2. **Skill comes from both hiring and experience:** hire at market pay; dispatchers and drivers level up through play.
3. **Staff are poachable** by other players.
4. **Driver turnover now, not later:** underpaid or badly treated staff quit; all the labor dynamics ship together.

Agreed design (from the proposal Jeff accepted): dispatcher skill = forecast horizon on the season curves (pre-positioning), market read (noise/lag), bid accuracy, load selection incl. reload odds, span of control (~5 trucks low → 15–20 high). Driver skill = ±10% mpg, ±20% maintenance/breakdowns, ±5% miles/day, fewer late deliveries; drivers gain experience by miles; the owner-operator is their own driver. Finite NPC labor pool, 1–5 stars, pay scales with skill (drivers ~0.65–0.95 FD/mi; dispatchers salary or 5–8% of gross), weekly hire/fire. Wages should roughly offset fuel/repair savings for the median company so total sinks (and the FD price) stay neutral; skill must stay scarce so the seasonal edge isn't competed away. Validate with the 10-seed sims (liquidations, strategy balance vs 3×, token price, value of a skilled hire).

Proposed next layer, Jeff asked for opinions only so far: safety/compliance and fraud as the "degen" element — HOS/speed violations for hot loads, maintenance shortcuts, CSA-style safety score, police stops and weigh-station inspections weighted by score, fines, out-of-service orders and shutdowns, double brokering, freight theft, and broker/shipper vetting of carriers (spec already has double-brokering penalties and adversarial bot personas).

Jeff's answers on the safety/fraud layer (2026-10-28, build approved):
- Theft: players can steal from other players and from AI shippers/brokers, both from the start.
- Crashes: as punishing as the real thing (semi crashes often kill people); severity of punishment depends on the cause (not at fault < at-fault accident < negligence < willful falsified logs / known defects).
- Shutdown: Jeff left it to Claude. Chosen: tiered, timed bars on the account (not permanent, since permanent bans just push people to new wallets): unsatisfactory rating → authority revoked + 30-day bar; imminent hazard → 180 days; criminal (fatal crash with willful violations, or theft conviction) → cash seized, 365-day bar, complicit driver/dispatcher jailed; repeat criminal → permanent. Everything forfeited (fleet, Miles, airdrop); the record follows the account (chameleon flag for brokers' vetting).

- **Starting-hub incentive = shipper leverage.** No backhaul edge (inbound FL rates are already high). The fewer trucks per load a market has (shippers' leverage low) the more its shippers open to outside carriers; the more trucks per load (Miami, trucks pile up) the more shippers work exclusively with local carriers. Model it as a local-first tendering share from the market's load-to-truck ratio, in every market, plus contracts: local shippers offer contract lanes at a fixed rate (priced off the lane's annual average); when spot spikes (FL produce season) the owner chooses between servicing contracts and chasing spot; tender rejection costs trust, volume and the relationship that earns the exclusive tenders.
- **Book-now price** on every load, as proposed: poster sets it from market leverage, steps up each unfilled round, carriers take instantly or wait; dispatcher/bot order with a desperation-driven threshold; sealed-bid rounds stay. Needs an away-from-home cost (per diem, driver morale) so visiting trucks are desperate and locals are patient.
- **Driver fatigue:** more than 3 weeks on the road builds fatigue that affects everything; 5 days off resets it and extra days off give a small buffer; driving more than 10 h a day builds fatigue too, compounding the more it happens; fatigue raises crash risk etc.
- **Places/nodes:** Jeff asked whether HERE maps (Trucking Terminal uses it for TMS routing) should supply maps/routing/places. Claude's answer: not inside the simulation (deterministic lockstep can't depend on a live API; terms limit storing results); use public data (Census places/population, BLS QCEW industry employment, USDA NASS crops, FAF5 commodity flows) for places and flows; HERE optionally offline to calibrate the road graph's speeds/tolls, and later as the TMS bridge.
