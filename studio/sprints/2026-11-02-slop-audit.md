## The producer's one page for Jeff (2026-11-01, late; the four drafts are on main)

**What all four say, independently.** The game plays itself: the default dispatcher bids, wins and sends a new
player's only truck away before they have read the board, and the walkthrough ticks its own boxes. The token is
on screen in the first two minutes (the Money tab's auction and airdrop estimate, the header's launch counter, the
Dealer's dollar prices) a day after Miles became "standing, never a promised conversion". Thirteen tabs, wordless
on a laptop and clipped on a phone. On a phone half the screen is chrome. The walkthrough is the best writing in
the build and it gets in the way: a wall of text above every tab, Iuliia at 64 px, narrating a game that isn't on
screen. Money is a mystery: revenue and costs with no ledger, no receivables, no due days. What's good is the
spine: the market over real roads, the load detail with break-even and "usually clears near", the honest bid
toasts, the ticker, the title painting.

**What sprint 1 would be, if Jeff says yes to the bible's questions.** The first ten minutes by hand: a
one-truck company starts with the dispatcher silent until the player has placed a bid (a rule change, the one
economy move); four tabs on a phone, Truck · Board · Money · More, the rest appearing on the day they matter;
load cards with one profit chip instead of the DAT table on a phone; a bid that leaves a pinned card and a dashed
lane on the map instead of a toast, and a result card when the round clears; a Money tab that is a ledger with
receivables and due days; Iuliia a sentence or two at a time, her face large, in one palette and two typefaces;
the token surfaces hidden until counsel and the server. Engineering says what's hard to change once its note is
in; the writer's copy audit sets the words.

**Decided by the drafts, for Jeff to confirm:** the theme picker goes; the Back Lot teaser leaves the beta's tab
bar and lives on the roadmap page (the art guide and the director agree, against the teaser idea of yesterday).
